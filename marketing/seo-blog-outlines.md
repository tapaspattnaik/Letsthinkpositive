# 4 SEO Blog Post Outlines — 2 Months of Content

> Publish cadence: 1 post every 2 weeks. Each post targets one primary keyword with realistic difficulty for a new domain, links to 2–3 site tools (the conversion path), and is designed to earn Pinterest saves.
>
> Writing rules for every post:
> - Primary keyword in: title, H1, first 100 words, one H2, URL slug, meta description
> - 1,500–2,200 words (long enough to compete, short enough to actually write)
> - Every claim gets a source link (psychology journals, Sleep Foundation, APA) — E-E-A-T signals
> - End with a soft CTA box to the relevant free tool
> - Add FAQ section with 3–4 questions (targets People Also Ask + featured snippets)

## Post 1 — "30 Gratitude Journal Prompts for When You Don't Know What to Write"

- **Primary keyword:** gratitude journal prompts (high volume, list-intent — winnable with a genuinely better list)
- **Secondary:** gratitude prompts for adults, daily gratitude questions
- **Slug:** `/blog/gratitude-journal-prompts`
- **Meta description:** "30 gratitude journal prompts to beat the blank page — organised by mood, with tips to make gratitude a daily habit that sticks. Free journal included."

### Outline
1. **Intro (100w):** The blank-page problem — you sit down to journal and your mind empties. Prompts fix this.
2. **Why prompts work (150w):** Reduces activation energy; cite research on "3 good things" practice (Seligman 2005)
3. **The 30 prompts, grouped by situation (core content, ~900w):**
   - 6 prompts for ordinary days ("What smell made you pause today?")
   - 6 for hard days (gentler: "What's one thing that didn't get worse today?")
   - 6 for relationships ("Who showed up for you this month?")
   - 6 for self-appreciation ("What did your body do for you today?")
   - 6 for the past & future ("What were you worried about a year ago that resolved?")
4. **How to actually build the habit (300w):** Anchor to existing routine, keep it to one sentence, track a streak — natural segue to the app's journal + streak features
5. **FAQ:** How long should I journal? Morning or night? What if I repeat answers? Does it really work?
6. **CTA box:** "Try these prompts in our free gratitude journal — smart prompts adapt to your mood, streaks keep you going."

**Internal links:** `/journal`, `/challenges` (30-day gratitude), `/mood`
**Pinterest angle:** the 30 prompts become 5 list-pins (6 prompts each)

## Post 2 — "The 4-7-8 Breathing Technique: How It Works and When to Use It"

- **Primary keyword:** 4-7-8 breathing technique (steady volume, clear how-to intent)
- **Secondary:** breathing exercises for anxiety, breathing technique for sleep
- **Slug:** `/blog/4-7-8-breathing-technique`
- **Meta description:** "Learn the 4-7-8 breathing technique step by step — why it calms anxiety fast, when to use it, and common mistakes. Includes a free guided breathing tool."

### Outline
1. **Intro (100w):** The one tool you always have with you. 2 minutes, no equipment, works in a meeting or at 3am.
2. **What it is (150w):** Dr. Andrew Weil's adaptation of pranayama — inhale 4, hold 7, exhale 8
3. **Step-by-step guide (300w):** Posture, tongue position, the counts, 4 cycles max for beginners (lightheadedness warning — this builds trust)
4. **The science (300w):** Extended exhale → vagus nerve → parasympathetic activation; heart-rate variability; cite at least 2 studies
5. **When to use it (250w):** Falling asleep, pre-presentation nerves, anger interrupts, panic onset — concrete scenarios
6. **Common mistakes (200w):** Forcing the counts, chest breathing vs belly, expecting instant magic
7. **Alternatives if 4-7-8 feels hard (150w):** Box breathing, simple long-exhale — internal link to breathing tool which offers multiple patterns
8. **FAQ:** How many times a day? Is it safe for kids? Why do I feel dizzy? 4-7-8 vs box breathing?
9. **CTA box:** "Practice with our free visual breathing guide — animated pacing, calming sounds, no signup."

**Internal links:** `/breathing`, `/sounds`, `/sleep`
**Pinterest angle:** step-by-step infographic pin (these dominate the breathing niche)

## Post 3 — "Why Can't I Sleep? 7 Science-Backed Fixes for Racing Thoughts at Night"

- **Primary keyword:** racing thoughts at night (strong problem-intent, lower competition than "insomnia")
- **Secondary:** can't sleep because of overthinking, how to quiet your mind to sleep
- **Slug:** `/blog/racing-thoughts-at-night`
- **Meta description:** "Mind racing at bedtime? 7 science-backed ways to quiet overthinking and fall asleep faster — from cognitive shuffling to the worry journal technique."

### Outline
1. **Intro (120w):** It's 1am, body exhausted, brain hosting a conference. Validate the experience first.
2. **Why your brain does this (200w):** First unstimulated moment of the day; cortisol rhythm; the bed-as-worry-trigger association
3. **The 7 fixes (core, ~1,100w):**
   1. The worry journal — scheduled worry time before bed (cite Penn State study)
   2. Cognitive shuffling — random word imagery to derail linear thought
   3. The 4-7-8 breath (internal link to Post 2 — cluster building)
   4. Body scan meditation
   5. Sound masking — pink noise/rain (link `/sounds`)
   6. The 15-minute rule — leave bed, dim light, boring activity
   7. Fix the daytime inputs — caffeine cutoff, screen sunset, consistent wake time
4. **When to seek help (150w):** >3 nights/week for >3 months → talk to a doctor; mention CBT-I as gold standard (honesty builds E-E-A-T)
5. **Track it to fix it (200w):** You can't improve what you don't measure — sleep tracker + mood correlation feature as the natural tool
6. **FAQ:** Is melatonin worth it? Why do I wake at 3am? Does counting sheep work?
7. **CTA box:** "Try the free 21-Day Sleep Reset challenge — gentle nightly habits, a tracker, and calming sounds in one place."

**Internal links:** `/sleep`, `/sounds`, `/challenges` (sleep-21), `/journal`, blog Post 2
**Pinterest angle:** "7 fixes" list pin + standalone "cognitive shuffling" explainer pin (novelty = saves)

## Post 4 — "Positive Affirmations That Actually Work (And Why Most Don't)"

- **Primary keyword:** do positive affirmations work (question-intent — featured-snippet target)
- **Secondary:** how to write affirmations, affirmations that work
- **Slug:** `/blog/positive-affirmations-that-work`
- **Meta description:** "Do affirmations actually work? The research says yes — but only if you avoid 3 common mistakes. How to write affirmations your brain will believe."

### Outline
1. **Intro (100w):** Contrarian hook — "I am a money magnet" said into a mirror does nothing. Here's what does.
2. **The honest science (300w):** Self-affirmation theory (Steele); fMRI studies on self-processing regions; the Wood et al. 2009 finding that unbelievable affirmations can *backfire* for low self-esteem — leading with the counter-evidence builds enormous trust
3. **Why generic affirmations fail (250w):** Cognitive dissonance — the brain rejects claims that contradict self-image
4. **The 3 rules for affirmations that work (450w):**
   1. Believable > aspirational ("I am learning to stay calm" vs "I am always calm")
   2. Values-based > outcome-based (affirm who you are, not what you'll get)
   3. Specific + present-tense action ("I take one small step even when anxious")
5. **A simple formula + 15 examples (300w):** "I [present-tense verb] [specific behaviour] [even when/because]" — grouped for stress, confidence, health
6. **Make it a practice (200w):** Morning anchor, written > spoken, rotate weekly — segue to AI-personalised daily affirmation feature
7. **FAQ:** How long until affirmations work? Should I say them aloud? How many at once?
8. **CTA box:** "Get a fresh affirmation each morning, personalised to how you're actually feeling — free."

**Internal links:** `/affirmation`, `/intention`, `/reframe`, `/quotes`
**Pinterest angle:** the 15 example affirmations become 3 quote-card pins + 1 "formula" infographic
