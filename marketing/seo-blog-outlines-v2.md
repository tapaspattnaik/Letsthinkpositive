# 5 SEO Blog Post Outlines — Batch 2 (Global Long-Tail)

> Audience: global English (US, UK, Europe, ASEAN, ANZ). Each post targets a high-intent, evergreen long-tail query that big wellness apps under-serve, links to 2–3 free tools (the conversion path), and is built to earn Pinterest saves + featured snippets.
>
> Writing rules (same as Batch 1):
> - Primary keyword in: title, H1, first 100 words, one H2, URL slug, meta description
> - 1,500–2,200 words; every claim gets a credible source link (APA, Sleep Foundation, NIH, peer-reviewed) for E-E-A-T
> - End with a soft CTA box to the relevant free tool
> - FAQ section with 3–4 questions (targets People Also Ask)
> - No country-specific framing — examples and language stay globally neutral

## Post 5 — "How to Stop Overthinking at Night: 8 Techniques That Actually Work"

- **Primary keyword:** how to stop overthinking at night (high volume, high intent, winnable long-tail)
- **Secondary:** racing thoughts at night, can't sleep because of overthinking, quiet your mind before bed
- **Slug:** `/blog/how-to-stop-overthinking-at-night`
- **Meta description:** "Can't switch your brain off at night? 8 evidence-based techniques to stop overthinking and fall asleep faster — plus a free breathing tool to try right now."

### Outline
1. **Intro (100w):** The 2 a.m. mental spiral — why the brain ramps up rumination when the day goes quiet.
2. **Why your brain overthinks at night (200w):** Reduced distraction + cortisol/circadian factors; cite Sleep Foundation on pre-sleep arousal.
3. **The 8 techniques (core, ~1,000w), each with how + why:**
   - 4-7-8 breathing (down-regulates the nervous system) → link `/breathing`
   - "Worry window" / scheduled worry time (CBT)
   - Brain-dump journaling before bed → link `/journal`
   - Cognitive defusion — name the thought, don't argue with it → link `/reframe`
   - 5-4-3-2-1 grounding
   - Progressive muscle relaxation
   - Reframe the "I must sleep now" pressure
   - Consistent wind-down routine → link `/sleep`
4. **What NOT to do (150w):** doomscrolling, clock-watching, "trying harder" to sleep.
5. **FAQ:** Why do I overthink only at night? Is it anxiety or normal? Should I get out of bed? Do breathing exercises really help?
6. **CTA box:** "Next time your mind races, open our free guided breathing tool — no app, no signup."

**Internal links:** `/breathing`, `/journal`, `/reframe`, `/sleep`
**Pinterest angle:** "8 ways to stop overthinking at night" checklist pin.

## Post 7 — "What to Do When You Feel Behind in Life (You're Not as Far Back as You Think)"

- **Primary keyword:** feeling behind in life (high emotional-search volume, low commercial competition)
- **Secondary:** what to do when you feel behind, comparison anxiety, everyone is ahead of me
- **Slug:** `/blog/what-to-do-when-you-feel-behind-in-life`
- **Meta description:** "Feeling behind in life? You're measuring against the wrong timeline. A compassionate, practical guide to comparison anxiety — with reframing tools that help."

### Outline
1. **Intro (120w):** The universal lie of the "timeline" — milestones by 30, etc. Validate the feeling first.
2. **Why we feel behind (200w):** Social comparison theory (Festinger); social media's highlight reel; cite research on comparison + wellbeing.
3. **5 reframes (core, ~900w):**
   - There is no universal timeline (life paths aren't linear)
   - You compare your inside to others' outside → link `/reframe`
   - "Behind" implies a single race — name your actual goals → link `/journal`
   - Progress is invisible day-to-day — track it → link `/mood`
   - Redefine "on time" as "in motion"
4. **A 3-step practice for comparison spirals (250w):** notice → name → narrow focus to your next small step.
5. **FAQ:** Is it normal to feel behind? How do I stop comparing myself? Does everyone feel this way? How do I find my own pace?
6. **CTA box:** "When the comparison spiral hits, reframe it with our free thought-reframing tool."

**Internal links:** `/reframe`, `/journal`, `/coach`
**Pinterest angle:** quote-style pins ("There is no timeline. There is only your next step.").

## Post 8 — "20 Journaling Prompts for Processing Difficult Emotions"

- **Primary keyword:** journaling prompts for emotions (list-intent, winnable)
- **Secondary:** journal prompts for anxiety, prompts for processing feelings, expressive writing prompts
- **Slug:** `/blog/journaling-prompts-for-difficult-emotions`
- **Meta description:** "20 journaling prompts to process difficult emotions — anger, grief, anxiety, and overwhelm — grouped by feeling, with a free journal to write in right now."

### Outline
1. **Intro (100w):** Naming a feeling reduces its intensity ("affect labeling") — journaling is the tool.
2. **Why expressive writing works (150w):** Cite Pennebaker's expressive-writing research + affect-labeling (UCLA fMRI studies).
3. **The 20 prompts, grouped by emotion (core, ~1,000w):**
   - 5 for anxiety/overwhelm ("What is the worry under the worry?")
   - 5 for anger/frustration ("What boundary was crossed?")
   - 5 for sadness/grief ("What does this loss say about what mattered?")
   - 5 for stuck/numb ("If this feeling could speak, what would it ask for?")
4. **How to journal safely with hard feelings (250w):** time-box it, end on a grounding line, when to seek support.
5. **FAQ:** Does journaling help anxiety? What if writing makes me feel worse? How often? Typed or handwritten?
6. **CTA box:** "Write through it in our free private journal — guided prompts adapt to your mood."

**Internal links:** `/journal`, `/mood`, `/reframe`
**Pinterest angle:** 4 pins, one per emotion group (5 prompts each).
